## Central idea

**A gap is safe only in relation to what happens next.** The player chooses a destination, the program checks the traffic already there, then traffic advances and the program checks again. Waiting can be a useful decision on safe ground and a losing decision in a lane. Multiple predictable lanes turn this small rule into a planning problem.

Keep three representations distinct: logical cells describe the rules, lane phases and countdowns describe change, and character strings draw the result. Neither a picture nor its colour determines contact. The player needs to read these relationships; the learner needs to explain them.

Begin a new program. Present the first short listing in full without a redundant instruction to add every numbered line. Recall coordinates, variables, loops, subroutines, arrays and UDGs locally. No earlier game's engine is required. Subsequent checkpoints need exact add/replace/delete instructions, including temporary inspection controls that are later removed.

## Derive the representations

Use the game's actual coordinates in diagrams and trace tables. A logical cell (x,y) starts at character row `2+2*y`, column `1+2*x`. The second printed row is one character row below. The final player at (7,8) therefore occupies character rows 18–19 and columns 15–16. The 15×9 field spans 30×18 character cells, or 240×144 pixels. PRINT AT uses row before column; the game's x/y names do not change that order.

Lane phase p identifies one vehicle start on a 15-cell ring. Vehicle starts are p, p+5 and p+10, wrapped to the board; each occupies its start and next cell. Derive occupancy first by enumerating these cells, then explain the accepted expression `delta = nx-p(lane)` followed by `delta = delta-5*INT(delta/5)`. The result is 0..4 even when the initial difference is negative. Values 0 and 1 are vehicle cells; 2, 3 and 4 are gaps. For example, phase 14 occupies cells 14,0,4,5,9,10. This modulo-five rule depends on this particular repeated pattern; it is not a general vehicle collision algorithm.

Keep the 15-cell phase wrap, five-cell repeating pattern and 30-character string rotation distinct. Draw how a cell crossing the right edge reappears on the left. Explain one-based string positions and inclusive slices before the rotation expressions. Fixed-length string arrays give each lane a 30-character row; numeric arrays hold different information.

In the full board, reject safe rows 0,4,8 before indexing a lane. Rows 1–3 map directly to lane indexes 1–3; rows 5–7 map to indexes 4–6 by subtracting one. The lane table is the level design:

| Lane | Logical row | Initial phase | Direction | Updates per movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 0 | Right (+1) | 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 9 | Left (−1) | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 11 | Right (+1) | 2 |
| 4 | 5 | 7 | Left (−1) | 3 |
| 5 | 6 | 1 | Right (+1) | 4 |
| 6 | 7 | 10 | Left (−1) | 2 |

The one-lane trial uses the final bottom lane's phase, direction and period, placed at practice row 1. Expanding the board changes its row and adds the other five records; it does not introduce a different collision rule.

## Timing, input and drawing

Explain the order with a trace showing old traffic, candidate player position, accepted player position and new traffic. A vehicle about to leave a cell does not make that cell available early. Transparent corners are still part of the logical rectangle. Colour is presentation; changing INK must not change contact.

The 32-frame threshold spaces update starts. Drawing and interpreted BASIC work can exceed it, and the program does not run several catch-up steps afterwards. Lane periods count updates, not video frames. The low frame byte wraps at 256; the subtraction correction is suitable for this bounded loop, not a general timer for arbitrarily long pauses. Retain the measured endpoint cadence and its limits in the execution record; measure intermediate programs separately if prose compares their speed.

Only due lanes redraw. A resting player need not disappear while other lanes are printed. Moving the player erases its old background before drawing the new position; printing its lane requires restoring the overlay. Explain these choices alongside their visible effects, rather than describing them as an unexplained optimisation pass.
