## What the learner brings

The current route is Meet BASIC → Bright Spark → Volley → Touchdown → Sonar → Crates. Start a new program; reuse explained ideas and graphics techniques rather than editing Sonar into a different game.

The authored Touchdown lessons introduce INKEY$ in unit 3, release handling in unit 4, DATA/READ/RESTORE and a terrain array in units 8–9, and UDG row bytes, USR with a string argument, POKE and CHR$ in unit 10. Sonar units 5–6 develop two-dimensional arrays and reconstructing a display from remembered state. These are observed teaching precedents, not proof that every reader remembers them. Recall the required contracts locally and link to those explanations where useful.

Crates extends this work: four characters make one square tile; the array describes the world rather than knowledge about it; a push changes two cells together; targets survive objects moving over them; and readable strings describe restartable rooms. Explain string arrays and slicing locally. Do not call DATA, custom characters or polling first-time subjects on this route, and do not assume attendance at Cipher or the legacy Crates course.

The central rule is **inspect the proposed move, decide whether it is legal, then change the state**. A rejected move changes nothing. Drawing presents that state and cannot decide whether a move is legal.

## Deliberate differences from the prototype

The prototype constructs room one with assignments and modifies that initial layout for rooms two and three. The teaching endpoint decodes and validates explicit maps. Executed checks and an independent map audit compare each loaded grid and player coordinate with the accepted prototype. Final source bytes therefore need not equal the prototype, but its playable rules, maps, graphics and controls must remain equivalent unless a change is explicitly agreed.

The implemented map alphabet is `-`, `#`, `.`, `C`, `P`, `*` and `+` for floor, wall, target, crate, player, crate-on-target and player-on-target respectively. Rows use eight visible symbols; spaces are not accepted or silently trimmed. Keep validation proportional to the format actually taught. There is no level editor, automatic deadlock detection, solver or undo system in the learner's game.

The accepted prototype marks a delivered crate by changing its colour. Do not claim its graphics already provide a non-colour distinction for every state. The teaching source adds a quiet GOALS filled/total readout as an alternative cue. The executed readout captures were included in the approved website review. This is not a separate native playtest of the teaching endpoint. The player-on-target ring already preserves that underlying target visually.

## Questions worth asking

- Why check the cell beyond a crate before changing its current cell? A rejected push must leave both cells and the player unchanged; otherwise the program has to undo a partial move.
- Does a legal push guarantee a solvable room? No. Room two supplies an executed counterexample. The collision rule answers whether this move is allowed, not whether future play can finish.
- Why is moving between two targets not a change in the uncovered-target count? Leaving exposes one target; entering covers another. Both changes matter even though their total is zero.
- Why can the player remain outside the grid while a crate-on-target needs its own code? The player has a separate location and can be overlaid. Crates occupy stored world cells, so their codes must retain the underlying target.
- Does a valid map mean a good puzzle? No. Counts and symbols do not establish solvability, readable choices or enjoyment. Solve it and play it.
- After one delivery in room three, why is the room still active? One target remains uncovered. Completion depends on the whole room, not the most recent successful push.

Supply reasoning through the shared Question presentation. Predictions and traces should use actual maintained checkpoints. Optional level edits should ask the learner to predict access, find a solution and compare the result, not demand the author's shortest route or introduce a compulsory par.
