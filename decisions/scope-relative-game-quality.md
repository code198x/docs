# Decision: Game quality is relative to the agreed scope

**Status:** Active. Agreed 2026-09-05.

## The decision

Every curriculum game must be complete, enjoyable, readable, responsive and deliberately finished for its agreed scope. Commercial scale or historical saleability is not a universal requirement. This applies across tracks; BASIC games in particular do not need to meet a commercial standard.

A small game can fulfil its promise with one room, one mechanic or a short session. More levels, more opponents, longer music and more elaborate rendering are justified by the experience and teaching they enable, not by matching a commercial release's feature count.

Selected later projects may retain substantial commercial ambition. Their briefs must name that ambition and explain why it serves the project. Assembly does not automatically imply a commercial-scale game, and this decision does not automatically reduce any existing game's agreed scope.

## What a brief must establish

- **The experience:** what the player does, why the decisions or interactions are worth exploring, and the intended session length.
- **The finished scope:** the rules, content, controls, feedback and ending or repeatable play cycle that fulfil that promise.
- **The teaching:** how programming, design, graphics and sound develop together, at a depth appropriate to the game and target.
- **The checks:** observable behaviour for rules, boundaries, restart and completion where applicable; playtesting of readability, responsiveness and enjoyment.

Graphics, sound, level design and polish remain deliberate design responsibilities. Each needs an appropriate treatment, not an equal quantity of content. Deliberate silence, a compact visual vocabulary or a single carefully designed puzzle can be valid choices.

Runnable intermediate stages may be incomplete while the learner builds the game. A deliverable described as a finished game must fulfil its stated scope; essential work cannot be deferred while claiming completion.

## Verification and comparisons

Existing applicable correctness and end-to-end verification requirements remain. A smaller scope is not a reason to skip testing. Record which behaviour was checked by execution, which emulator or hardware configuration was used, and what remains unverified. Playtesting answers questions that successful builds and scripted runs cannot settle.

Historical games remain useful evidence, inspiration and optional comparators. Commercial price tiers, feature censuses and parity with a named release are not universal completion tests. Use a comparator to investigate a specific design or implementation question when it helps.

## Why

A universal commercial bar makes scale stand in for quality and can inflate an early teaching project beyond its purpose. The curriculum should support small, satisfying finished games as well as ambitious later work. The standard is the quality of the experience promised, not how much content surrounds it.

## Relationship to existing guidance

This decision supersedes universal commercial, budget-label and full-price requirements in `commercial-bar-revamp.md`, `real-retro-games.md`, `curriculum-structure.md`, `phase-boundaries.md`, and dependent specifications or briefs. Their applicable commitments to guided progression, multi-disciplinary design, correctness and finished deliverables remain.

`game-completeness-bar.md` remains a proposal, not an adopted policy. Its universal commercial-comparator gate is not adopted by this decision.

Game selections, route order, target configurations and detailed lesson specifications remain separate decisions. Approval of this quality bar does not approve the provisional lineups from the review.
