# Image sources for Hamilton Johnson and Vaughan

The strongest first selection is Katherine Johnson at her desk in 1966, with NASA and photographer Bob Nye named in both the catalogue and file metadata. Hamilton’s code-stack photograph is a promising noncommercial licence lead whose live catalogue terms need rechecking. Vaughan’s retirement photographs carry a family credit and need a separate permission route. An alternative young Vaughan portrait has unresolved provenance.

This shortlist supports [imagery task #801](https://github.com/code198x/website/issues/801) and writing issues [Hamilton #782](https://github.com/code198x/website/issues/782), [Johnson #783](https://github.com/code198x/website/issues/783) and [Vaughan #784](https://github.com/code198x/website/issues/784). The [structured records](shortlist.json) include image URLs, dimensions, captions, alt text, credits, preview hashes and next actions. No shortlisted image has been added to the public website or bundled in this repository.

## Katherine Johnson at her desk

**Preferred source:** [NASA item LRC-1966-B701_P-06717](https://images.nasa.gov/details/LRC-1966-B701_P-06717), supported by the [API item record](https://images-api.nasa.gov/search?nasa_id=LRC-1966-B701_P-06717) and [file metadata](https://images-assets.nasa.gov/image/LRC-1966-B701_P-06717/metadata.json). The record dates the photograph to **8 September 1966** at NASA Langley Research Center and names **NASA, Bob Nye**. The [local provenance extract](johnson-source-metadata.json) retains those source fields.

**Credit:** `NASA / Bob Nye`.

**Reuse basis:** NASA-origin editorial imagery under the [NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/), rather than a Creative Commons licence. The file’s copyright field explicitly points to NASA’s guidance. Its copyright flag is set; that flag is not a third-party ownership identification and should not be silently replaced with a claim of CC0 or unrestricted reuse. The guidance permits factual educational/informational use with acknowledgement and without implied endorsement, and distinguishes third-party material. Promotional, merchandise and identifiable-person uses need their own consideration. This selection is for the historical article, not advertising.

**Proposed caption:** Katherine Johnson at her desk at NASA Langley Research Center, 8 September 1966.

**Proposed alt text:** Katherine Johnson seated at a desk with papers, a mechanical calculator and an open reference book.

The inspected JPEG is **1523 × 1920**; the listed TIFF’s metadata gives **3173 × 4000**. Use the full frame at roughly 450–600 CSS pixels wide, retaining the desk and working materials. NASA also hosts a separately sized/cropped rendition in its web gallery; do not confuse that rendition with this catalogue derivative. The scene supports an account of mathematical work, without claiming that the visible papers document a particular flight calculation.

**Disposition:** NASA editorial reuse basis documented. The project’s separate portrait-policy treatment remains to be resolved before publication.

## Margaret Hamilton with Apollo listings

**Preferred catalogue lead:** [MIT Museum GCP-00009689](https://mitmuseum.mit.edu/collections/object/GCP-00009689), titled *Margaret H. Hamilton with Apollo computer software, 1969*. The indexed institutional record describes the photograph as made at MIT in Cambridge in 1969. It states **CC BY-NC 4.0** and requires **“Courtesy MIT Museum.”**

**Proposed credit:** `Courtesy MIT Museum — CC BY-NC 4.0`, linked to the item and [licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/). Indicate any crop or other changes if made. The licence permits noncommercial sharing/adaptation with attribution; it does not establish that every Code198x placement qualifies or settle personality rights.

**Current verification limit:** The search-indexed MIT Museum item supplies the licence statement, but direct retrieval of the live page returned **HTTP 429 Too Many Requests** on 16 October 2026. The search index reported an approximately 1.3-year-old crawl. Treat the licence and required credit as a well-supported lead, not freshly confirmed permission. Revisit the same live record before obtaining its master or publishing it.

[NASA’s contextual article](https://science.nasa.gov/people/margaret-hamilton/) reproduces an account by Maia Weinstock identifying an unnamed Instrumentation Laboratory staff photographer, based on Hamilton’s recollection. NASA hosting that account or picture does not make it NASA-owned. The original team context matters: the stack represents Apollo flight-software listings produced by a team.

**Proposed caption:** Margaret Hamilton beside Apollo flight-software listings at MIT in 1969. The software was the work of a team, not Hamilton alone.

**Proposed alt text:** Margaret Hamilton standing beside a stack of bound software listings nearly as tall as she is.

A **552 × 690** preview from NASA’s contextual page was visually inspected. The MIT master’s dimensions are not established. Retain the full frame, obtain the museum source after its terms are confirmed, and avoid enlarging this small derivative. A display width around 400–550 CSS pixels preserves the relationship between Hamilton and the listings.

**Disposition:** Provisional licence lead. Recheck the live item, establish whether the intended placement meets NonCommercial or needs another permission route, and resolve project portrait policy.

## Dorothy Vaughan at her retirement party

**Preferred contextual source:** [NASA’s retirement-party article](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/dorothy-vaughans-retirement-party-1971/), published 1 December 2016, identifies the photographs as **1971** and gives the image credit **“Courtesy Vaughan Family.”** Its caption text is separately attributed to Margot Lee Shetterly. The photographer is not identified in that record.

**Proposed image credit, if authorised:** `Courtesy Vaughan Family; source: NASA`. Write our own caption rather than reproducing Shetterly’s text without checking its reuse basis.

**Rights:** The family credit is evidence of third-party provenance, not a licence. NASA’s own guidance says its permission to host third-party material does not pass reuse rights to others. Identify the rights holder and obtain or locate permission covering the intended article use before publishing. No request has been sent.

**Proposed caption:** Photographs from Dorothy Vaughan’s retirement party in 1971.

**Proposed alt text:** A montage of three black-and-white photographs of people gathered at Dorothy Vaughan’s retirement party.

The **1041 × 1008** supplied montage was inspected. It shows a celebration and social context rather than a computing task. Keep it intact at roughly 500–650 CSS pixels wide if permission is obtained. Do not extract a portrait or identify the other participants from appearance alone; establish identities and any cropping conditions first.

**Disposition:** Permission needed. The NASA record custodian or the family is the next research route for authorship, permission scope and identifications.

## Alternative Vaughan portrait

[NASA’s “Dorothy Vaughan in her twenties” record](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/dorothy-vaughan-in-her-twenties/) provides a **2376 × 3180** portrait. The inspected page establishes the subject and approximate life stage but supplies no photographer, rights holder, exact photo date or item-specific licence. Its 2017 upload path and 2023 page update are not photograph dates.

**Proposed caption:** Dorothy Vaughan in her twenties.

**Proposed alt text:** Black-and-white head-and-shoulders portrait of a young Dorothy Vaughan.

This is a useful likeness for an early-life passage, not evidence of her NACA/NASA workplace. Retain the whole portrait at about 350–450 CSS pixels wide if its rights are established. Do not label it `Photo: NASA`, or transfer the family credit from the separate retirement montage without evidence.

**Disposition:** Rights unresolved. Trace the original record and photographer/rights holder before choosing a credit or using the image.

## Publication conditions and verification

The binding family decision *Publish third-party imagery by class, and prefer the artefact we made ourselves* says: “Portraits are published only where the subject supplies or agrees to one.” The source research establishes possible copyright reuse routes; it does not establish subject agreement or amend that rule. Historical/deceased subjects and institutional licences still need an explicit policy disposition under #801 before website publication. This does not prevent completing the source shortlist or the text articles.

All four available derivatives were visually inspected without editing. Dimensions were read from the files, and SHA-256 hashes in the structured records identify exactly which derivatives were inspected. NASA’s item/API/file records and current guidance were retrieved on 16 October 2026. Hamilton’s museum terms retain the live-recheck qualification above. Names in captions follow the institutional identifications; no other pictured people have been identified by visual inference.

The research records, local links, identifiers, source dimensions and hashes were checked for consistency. No website manifest, schema, article, build or live publication changed in this sourcing pass. No third-party outreach was sent.
