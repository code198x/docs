# Status and availability claims audit

Audited 2026-09-23 against the website working tree (`Code198x/website`, branch `main`, including uncommitted changes). Local `main` is three commits ahead of `origin/main`: the four Game Feel lessons (`fc82081e`, `ed52f971`) are not yet published. The live site (code198x.com) was spot-checked with WebFetch and curl.

## Summary

The catalogue layer itself is consistent. Every `src/content/units/**` entry matches its `unit-NN.mdx` files one for one: no unit is marked available without a page, and no page lacks a catalogue entry. Every module directory appears in a module catalogue. No unit carries a future `pubDate`. Units without a `pubDate` still build, because the date only gates the RSS feed and the homepage "What's new" list. Nothing is hidden from learners by the publishing cron.

The problems are in the prose and templates around that layer. They fall into five groups:

1. **Planned lineups presented as coming.** 66 module pages have no lessons, yet they build publicly, appear in the sitemap and are served (HTTP 200). All 66 say "currently in development. Check back soon!". Many state fixed sizes ("64 units", "256 units", "512 units") and prerequisite chains that name games no catalogue contains. The C64, Amiga (AMOS, Blitz, assembly) and NES track pages list 81 "Coming soon" modules under "Start with Game 1 and work through in order". The Spectrum assembly lineup has no settled order, yet ten "in development" pages for it exist at public URLs. The charter says "no later lineup is settled" and "New game sequences remain proposals until agreed".
2. **Hard-coded counts that are wrong or tell the reader to finish a set.** Examples: "Complete these 8 games" on AMOS, which has no games; "dozens of games per machine" on the homepage; "Five CPUs" for Asm198x; "Six primary / 22 more" for Emu198x.
3. **Stale descriptions of the shared subjects.** Start Here, Teaching, Press and the Curriculum Map still call Maths for Games an outline. The live site has 11 Maths for Games lessons.
4. **Starting points that contradict the catalogue or the independent-entry rule.** The NES "Start" button skips Meet the Machine. The C64 assembly "Ready to start?" copy describes Starfield but links to Meet the Machine. Several pages call BASIC or AMOS a "gateway" to assembly, or send assembly readers to Foundations first.
5. **Support surfaces conflated or out of date.** The homepage says learners use native emulators, although the lessons embed Emu198x. The Family page says Play198x's build has not started, while the Colophon lists it as a working build tool. Game Gear gets a browser player although its system entry has `emu198x` false. One sentence says Emu198x accuracy justifies "works on real hardware".

Retired lessons: no standalone Story Builder, Lucky Number, Oracle Stone, Reflex, Hi-Lo or Quiz Master page remains in Spectrum BASIC. The Spectrum BASIC landing page correctly presents the first three as parts of Meet BASIC. The only leftovers are dead fallback keys on the CRASH! Live page. C64 BASIC has its own `reflex` (published) and `quizmaster` (planned). The charter retires those names from Spectrum BASIC only, so they are listed below at low severity for confirmation.

## Checked and consistent

- `src/content/units/**` agrees with `unit-*.mdx` for all 41 unit catalogues: every available unit has a page and every page is catalogued.
- Module `index.mdx` pages for published modules make no lesson-count claims that disagree with their files.
- No `unit-*.mdx` has a future `pubDate`. Units without a date (Spectrum BASIC games, Meet Assembly, Meteor Storm, Maths for Games) build normally; the date only affects RSS and the homepage.
- Spectrum BASIC and Spectrum assembly landing pages: correct starting points (Meet BASIC; Meet Assembly then Meteor Storm), independent entry stated, published modules only, and retired lessons described as part of Meet BASIC.
- `/systems`, `/systems/by-tier` and `systems/[platform].astro` distinguish lesson availability from catalogue priority, and state that a listing is not a course or toolchain.
- About, Standards, Setup and Contribute make no incorrect availability claims. The About sentence at `about.astro:46` links Game Feel without claiming lessons.
- CRASH! Live's claim that every Meteor Storm lesson "includes runnable source and a browser experiment" is supported: 24 of 24 units use `MeteorExperiment`.
- `src/pages/commodore-64/` and `src/pages/sinclair-zx-spectrum/` are empty directories, so there is nothing to audit there. Those routes are served under `/systems/`.
