# Adventure / Colossal Cave — second game study

**Expanded draft for owner review, 18 October 2026. Not integrated or deployed.** The owner requested a fairly comprehensive account of Adventure and text-adventure/parser principles after finding the first pass too brief. Wider connections develop from the game rather than forming a required itinerary.

Read [the study](study.md), then [evidence and limits](evidence.md). [Connections](connections.md) records useful existing destinations; [the named-subject audit](vault-audit.md) reuses the candidate backlog. The original [entrance observations](observations.json) and [script](observe.py) remain, alongside the broader [encounter records](deep-observations.json) and [repeatable encounter check](check-encounters.py). [The plan](../../plans/2026-10-18-adventure-study.md) bounds this work.

The main account now explains the player’s learning loop; vocabulary, partial commands, synonyms and grammar; object resolution and scope; error categories and feedback; locations, directed/conditional travel and object state; mapping and markers; worked puzzle dependencies; turns, light, carrying and chance; death, saving, scoring and closing. It also follows caving, collaborative credits, writing, access, distribution, commercial editions, authoring systems and testing. Three optional worked-puzzle disclosures isolate selected early/middle spoilers. The ending’s structure is explained without a winning command sequence or complete map.

The central executable remains a later C port of the 350-point game. Thirteen fresh-process encounters and seventeen outcome checks support selected behaviour; original FORTRAN/data and primary manuals/design documents supply further distinctions. Source inspection is identified where an entire system or ending has not been played. The later Inform and Zork comparisons do not silently add their capabilities to Adventure.

Three original diagram subjects each have wide/phone variants, captions and text equivalents: grate state, generic richer-parser flow, and a maze marker. They are explanations, not historical screenshots. Human reading/play, complete ending execution, original-hardware verification, proportionate terminal captures and the exact Level 9 manual issue remain open. No specific new lesson, remake, platform track, candidate batch or publication is commissioned.

Adventure game text is credited to Will Crowther and Don Woods; the observed modern port is by Arthur O’Dwyer. Short extracts illustrate criticism and review. All rights in the underlying game remain with their respective owners. No game source, binary, full transcript, audio, cave map or complete walkthrough is distributed. The original diagrams and explanatory examples are our own.
