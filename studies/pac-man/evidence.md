# Pac-Man — evidence and limits

19 October 2026. Research and visual draft, not historical sign-off.

## Original software observation

MAME 0.289 validates the held Midway arcade ROM set against its catalogue. Exact image/executable hashes and scripts are in `captures/manifest.json`. The first script supplies coin, start, left and up; it observes readiness, route clearing and score changes. The second supplies a lower-left route and captures the ghosts becoming blue after the pellet. Early route attempts which stopped against walls were adjusted by inspecting the actual maze, not by changing game memory. No cheats, ROM patches, invulnerability or scene reconstruction.

The included frames are native 224×288 portraits copied without alteration. No full maze, complete match, perfect score or 256th board was played. No observation of later games, home versions, physical joystick feel or real cabinet timing is claimed. Generic design scenarios and the two coordinate examples are our explanations, not transcripts of a historical session.

## Technical sources

Pittman’s Dossier is a primary reverse-engineering account. Use it for cornering, junction choices, modes, the upward-offset quirk and scoring. Its broader historical anecdotes and superlative claims are not adopted. Lomont’s emulation paper supports the individual target calculations and includes disassembly; its rough gather/scatter description is not used to settle the original schedule. The author’s main Pac-Man page says content was removed at Namco’s request; no ROM, protected disassembly or source scan is mirrored. The still-served paper is cited for factual analysis.

## Historical sources

The official chronology distinguishes the May location test from the July Japanese release. The team’s November 2020 interviews were published in English in June 2021; statements about influences, production and audience are attributed recollections. Lammers’ current author-hosted excerpt supplies the eating concept and design aims; no claim to read the now-truncated full interview is made. No first-ever power-up, first maze game, universal female preference or single-creator story is asserted.

The December 1980 Midway operator manual supplies cabinet, controls and settings evidence. Marketing claims are not treated as independently audited sales data. The later Microsoft announcement’s opening 1981 date is rejected in favour of the original release evidence; its description of the new timed game is used. Ms. Pac-Man comes through Golson’s participant presentation; no rights dispute is summarised without independent evidence. Later publisher descriptions establish releases and explicit connections, not our critical judgement from unseen play.
