## Evidence needed before a publishable study

1. **Archive comparison completed.** The 8,681-byte tape embedded in the held 3.1 module matches the archive member byte for byte. See captures/tape-identity.json. This verifies that module payload against the archive; the emulator and original hardware remain separate verification questions.
2. Run a two-person play/read session on the named version. Observe learning, aiming, readability, exhaustion/recovery and the selected star/hyperspace behaviour. Record participant observations as observations, not representative audience data.
3. **A complete scripted round is now recorded.** Both ships entered destruction, became inactive, then restarted with replenished torpedo counts; scores stayed zero. See captures/capture-report.json. It is a tie, not a positive-score test or a human match. Other configurations and all rare outcomes are not comprehensively tested.
4. Check source transcriptions against originals where available, including S1 pagination and any exact event chronology needed. Resolve acquisition details before using them.
5. Investigate audio/acoustic conditions before writing a historical sound account. The inspected browser engine contains no audio API references; that does not establish historical silence or the room's sound.
6. Identify period demonstrations and distribution material in more depth before quantifying reach, commercial effects or production resources. Do not invent a retail package for this game.
7. Choose permitted imagery and record source/credit/reuse terms under existing policy. Research screenshots are not article-ready publication assets. A CRT photograph, browser capture and illustrative diagram must remain distinguishable.
8. Human editorial review of the historical claims and design interpretation; then agree site placement and verify the rendered page. None of these steps changes the standing delivery queue automatically.

## Illustrated movement and controls revision

The movement sequence now defines velocity and acceleration, isolates coasting without gravity, then adds thrust and gravity. [NASA's explanation of Newton's laws](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/newtons-laws-of-motion/) supports the physical distinction. The four original diagrams are conceptual explanations; arrow lengths and time labels are illustrative, not measured game units or a claim about the original integrator or gravity formula. The vector generator checks equal coasting spacing, vector addition and the direction towards the star. Gravity's continued action is explicit when thrust is released.

The ships/torpedoes distinction remains sourced to S2. Treating it as a selected game rule is analysis; it does not establish the developers' undocumented intent or all later versions. General discussion of numerical models is explanation, not a reconstruction of Spacewar's calculations. The coordinate and fractional-movement links were checked against website origin/main's actual lessons and section routing. Live retrieval failed with `HTTP Error 403: Forbidden`; no live page check is claimed.

The controls comparison describes possible signals/mappings rather than a chronology of inventions. [W3C Gamepad](https://www.w3.org/TR/gamepad/) describes button states and graduated axes, [Pointer Lock](https://www.w3.org/TR/pointerlock/) describes relative movement, and [Logitech's wheel documentation](https://www.logitechg.com/en-us/products/driving/driving-force-racing-wheel.html) confirms position sensing and force feedback. These modern sources do not establish the construction of historical Spacewar controllers. The light-gun row describes the user action only; sensor operation, CRT timing and compatibility are not established here. Those would need a device-specific historical treatment. The original Russell recollection remains attributed separately.

All diagrams are original authoring work within the repository's existing licence scope. No historical image is copied. figures/generate.py regenerates the four SVGs without dependencies. Text alternatives and captions distinguish velocity arrows from trajectories and explain the artificial coasting case with gravity absent.

## Inspirations, culture and named subjects

The expanded cultural account separates Graetz's attributed recollection (S3, section I: reading Smith, Toho films, shared Hingham Street accommodation and the Harvard/MIT access contrast; section II: Wiitanen's military call-up) from our interpretation of the game. “Hingham Institute” is a humorous name for the accommodation, not a formal research institute. No specific Toho film screening or undocumented motive is inferred.

Additional primary work: [The Skylark of Space, 1928 serial text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20869/20869-h/20869-h.htm), title credit and chapter X, “The Rescue”. The title credits Edward Elmer Smith in collaboration with Lee Hawkins Garby. The chapter explicitly describes calculating both ships' speeds and applying deceleration before rendezvous. Our comparison with overshooting and braking in Spacewar is an editorial reading, not evidence that the developers copied that passage or used this edition. Graetz attests the series influence; this study does not independently establish which volumes or revisions they read. Garby's precise contribution needs separate biographical research before an entry is commissioned. The work's racial stereotypes and gender conventions must be considered if a fuller literary entry is developed; no stereotyped passage or period illustration is reproduced here.

The main collective-credit paragraph now retains Kotok, Piner and Saunders alongside the other contributors, following the program attribution documented in captures/README.md and the preservation host. Capture assets, arithmetic, configuration and the original/browser distinction are unchanged. The wider remit is the explicitly owner-authorised specifications/game-studies.md, not approval of the earlier separate voice-guidance proposal.

See [the Vault disposition audit](vault-audit.md) for checked coverage, candidates, alias distinctions, reference-only names and source leads.
